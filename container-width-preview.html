Container width — four options, measured live

Each panel renders the real css/styles.css — same header, services grid and split section — at the viewport you pick, scaled down to fit. The red bands are the empty side space; every number under a panel is read from the iframe's computed layout, not typed in by hand.

The question isn't "what max-width?" It's how much of the screen the text leaves empty. Today that gap grows with the monitor while the content stays fixed.

Scroll to the bottom panel and drag the slider to test any number between 1230 and 1320 yourself.

  • The gap is a percentage, not a number. A fixed 1230px box gives away 6% of a 1366 screen and 19% of a 1920 one. That is why it reads as "floating in the middle" only on big monitors.
  • 1260 is invisible. Five percentage points of nothing: 110px vs 125px at 1440, and it leaves 350px at 1920.
  • A fixes both ends. 1320/24 pulls laptops in to 47–84px, and the 1560 tier above 1700px halves the 1920 gap (365 → 204). The cost is visible in the panels: the split line reaches 78 characters at 1920, which is why the 62ch cap below is not optional.
  • B is too aggressive below 1500. At 1366 and 1440 the box is wider than the viewport, so only the 24px gutter is left — content touches the glass, and the 1920 gap is still 264px.
Now shipped in css/styles.css: --container:1320px and --gutter:24px on :root, with --gutter at 20px ≤820 and 16px ≤480, and --container:1560px from 1700px. Header and .w both consume the tokens, so the navbar can't drift from the section edge. The split-section prose is capped at 47ch (≈595px, ~68 characters) so the wide tier doesn't produce a 78-character line. Service cards measure 411px at 1320 and 491px at 1560.

Kept as the record of the comparison. All numbers above were measured from the real stylesheet at the selected viewport, including the header's fixed-position formula.
